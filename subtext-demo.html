Subtext

A caption editor for Premiere Pro. Import an SRT, refine the cut and the wording right in the panel, then push the result back to your timeline.

Getting started

  • Click Import, or drag a .srt, .vtt, or .csv file onto the panel (a CSV with frame timecodes asks for your frame rate once).
  • Already imported into Premiere? Drag the SRT from the Project panel onto Subtext.
  • Captions appear as editable lines, each with its timecode.

Editing captions

  • Single-click a line — moves Premiere's playhead to that caption.
  • ↑ / ↓ — walk the selection through the captions (the playhead follows); Enter edits the selected line; F flags it for review.
  • Double-click a word — edit just that word. Press Space while editing to split the line into two captions at the caret; Enter commits, Esc cancels.
  • Double-click the empty area of a line — edit the whole line as one field.
  • Click a “/” between words — split the line there. The trailing “/” merges a line with the next one.

Review & search

  • Flag for review — click the flag icon on a caption to mark it; a note field appears where you can jot why. Use the flag button in the toolbar to jump between flagged captions.
  • Find & Replace (magnifier, or Ctrl+F) — matches are highlighted as you type, with the first one selected; Enter / Shift+Enter step through them. Open the replace row to swap the selected match (Replace, then it moves to the next) or change every match at once (Replace all). Toggle Aa for case and W for whole-word (so “at” won’t match inside “procrastinating”). Flags and notes are Subtext-only and never touch the exported SRT.

Playback & sync

  • Play / Stop button or the Spacebar toggles timeline playback.
  • Sync highlights the caption under the playhead as the sequence plays.

History & output

  • History reopens your recent edits — work is auto-saved. On launch Subtext can reopen your previous session, start fresh, or ask (set it in ☰ → Settings → On launch).
  • Undo / Redo (or Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) adjust your edits. Panel size lives in Settings → UI → UI size.
  • Export SRT saves the edited file to disk (you choose where); Push to Premiere places the captions on a new caption track and files a versioned copy (named after your sequence, e.g. Sequence_v2.srt) in a Subtext bin in the Project panel. The SRT is saved to disk in a Subtext folder beside your project file — or set your own folder in ☰ → Settings → Caption save location.
